01 Profile

I learned finance the unglamorous way: reconciling cash, defending counts in audits, and explaining a bad week from a report nobody else had read closely.

Between June 2022 and March 2026 I went from sales associate to assistant store manager at Dollarama on Granville Street, a high-volume downtown Vancouver location with roughly 25 employees. The title says retail. The work was operational finance: daily sales and KPI reporting, cash reconciliation and bank deposits, inventory planning and purchasing, internal-control compliance, and inventory audits where the count either ties or you find out why.

Alongside that I completed my Bachelor of Commerce at University Canada West in July 2025, and I am now a CFA Program Candidate sitting Level I in February 2027. Coursework covered financial modelling and financial statement analysis; the CFA curriculum is where I am deepening valuation and portfolio work.

What I bring to a first analyst role is not a long list of models. It is four years of accountability for real money, a working habit of checking inputs before trusting output, and the leadership experience to communicate a finding to people who have to act on it.

02 How I think about analysis
01

Start at the discrepancy

A figure that does not tie is the most useful thing on the page. Cash variances and audit differences taught me to treat the exception as the entry point, not the annoyance.

02

A metric earns its place by changing a decision

Reporting nobody acts on is overhead. I would rather hand a manager the three drivers they can actually move than a dashboard of forty they cannot.

03

Controls before cleverness

Reconcile first, model second. An elegant analysis built on inputs nobody verified is a confident guess wearing a spreadsheet.
